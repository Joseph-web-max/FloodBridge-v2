# Related work and research-gap hypothesis

## Existing work identified
- **FloodNavigator (UC Berkeley MIMS project, 2025):** community-driven flood condition reporting, initially developed for Accra, Ghana. It is a direct neighbouring concept and demonstrates that crowdsourced flood reporting is not novel by itself.
- **Tana River Basin Flood Early Warning System (UNESCO, 2026):** UNESCO reported a basin flood early-warning system developed with ICHARM and the University of Tokyo, with institutional and mobile dissemination plans.
- **Nile Basin Initiative (2025 reporting):** reports development of a basin-wide web-based flash-flood early-warning system across Nile Equatorial Lakes countries.
- **WMO / UNDRR Early Warnings for All:** a global framework with disaster risk knowledge, monitoring/forecasting, warning communication and preparedness/response pillars. FloodBridge must complement, not displace, official warning chains.

## Proposed research question
Does recording provenance, freshness, contradictory crossing observations and manually mapped lifeline dependencies in a low-bandwidth workflow improve the speed and quality of local access-impact decisions compared with existing message / spreadsheet workflows?

## Potentially defensible contribution to investigate
A rigorous data-quality and operational protocol for converting heterogeneous community, sensor and official evidence into time-bounded crossing-state records, with traceable human decisions and explicit uncertainty propagation to linked essential-service assets.

This is a research hypothesis, not an established invention or a claim of patent novelty. A patentability and freedom-to-operate review requires a professional search of patents, papers, public-sector systems and commercial products.
