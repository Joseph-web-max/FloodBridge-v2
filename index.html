Route safety and limitations

This service calculates a road-network route using the configured routing provider. FloodBridge compares the route request with current incident records as a review aid. A point report does not define a closed road polygon, and a calculated route is never certified as safe.

Do not enter, cross or drive through floodwater. Confirm route conditions with competent local authorities before travel.

About

FloodBridge — evidence-to-access intelligence

FloodBridge is a decision-support layer that links crossing observations and water-level measurements to manually mapped dependencies for health facilities, shelters, schools, water points and other essential services.

Problem hypothesis

Communities and response teams may receive hazard messages but still lack timely, location-specific evidence about which crossings and essential-service connections are affected.

Proposed contribution

Track report freshness, contradictory evidence, instrument provenance and manually mapped lifeline dependencies in one low-bandwidth workflow.

Initial users

County disaster teams, road agencies, community responders, utilities, health networks, schools and humanitarian logistics teams.

Research caution

Flood early-warning services and community flood reporting already exist. FloodNavigator in Accra is an example of a community-driven flood-information platform. Kenya's Tana River Basin has a UNESCO-supported flood early-warning system. FloodBridge's differentiator is therefore a hypothesis around auditable crossing evidence and lifeline-access dependencies—not a claim that flood alerts or community maps are new.

Potential institutional outreach

Potential discussion targets include JKUAT, Kenya Meteorological Department, Water Resources Authority, Kenya National Highways Authority, Kenya Urban Roads Authority, county disaster-management teams, Kenya Red Cross, humanitarian mapping groups and local universities. These are prospects only; no relationship or endorsement is implied.

Operational boundaries

  • No official warning issuance or automatic connection to national emergency dispatch.
  • No automatic safe-route certification or road-closure authority.
  • No flood depth inference from rainfall alone.
  • When configured, route planning returns a baseline road-network route; it does not guarantee a flood-safe route or automatically remove every flooded segment.
  • Community reports can be unverified, wrong, stale, duplicated or malicious; review before acting.
  • Hydrological thresholds must be site-specific and approved by competent authorities.
